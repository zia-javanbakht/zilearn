## Comparison between numerical and analytical results

In this section, the results obtained from the proposed numerical model are compared with those predicted by the analytical model explained in Appendix A.

The analytical model proposed four scenarios for failure of the composite, and the third and fourth scenarios can be recognized as pseudo-ductility behavior of the composite. This study will focus on modelling the failure behavior shown in the third scenario. The tensile stress-strain curves were obtained from the analytical model (Appendix A) for two three-ply composites with middle ply fiber volume fractions of 30% and 40%. The equations were proposed by Chamis<sup>29</sup> were employed to obtain the elastic modulus ($E_c$) and tensile strength ($\sigma$) of each ply, which calculates the properties of each ply based on the volume fraction of each component (Equations (5) and (6)).

$$E_c = E_f V_f + E_m \left(1 - V_f\right) \tag{5}$$

$$\sigma_c = \sigma_f V_f \tag{6}$$

where, c, f, and m subscripts represent the composite ply, fiber, and matrix, respectively. The values of each parameter obtained using Chamis's model are shown in Table 3.

To observe a pseudo-ductile behavior in a hybrid composite, it is essential to assess the following criterion as outlined in<sup>8</sup>.

$$G_{II} = \frac{\epsilon_{Lb}^{2} E_L t_L \left(2E_H t_H + E_L t_L\right)}{8 E_H t_H} < G_{IIC} \tag{7}$$

In equation (7), $G_{II}$ and $G_{IIC}$ represent the energy release rates during the failure of the low-strain ply and the fracture toughness of mode II interface, respectively. $\epsilon_{Lb}$ denotes the failure strain of the low-strain ply.
